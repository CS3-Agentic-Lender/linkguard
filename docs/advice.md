# Advice text

This page lists every line of advice LinkGuard shows after a scan, with at least one official source for each. The advice panel and the "Where to get help" page copy their text from here word for word, so change both together.

The wording is ours. The substance comes from the sources, Irish first. Each source was opened and read on 12 November 2026, unless the table under "Sources" says otherwise.

## Every result, including Safe

| Line | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| Don't enter passwords or bank details from a link in a message you weren't expecting. Go to the company's website or app yourself instead. | [G1], [G3], [F1], [N1] |
| Your bank will never ask for your PIN or your full password in a text, an email or a phone call. | [G3], [F1], [N1] |

## Safe

The caveat under a Safe result is defined in the result card story's Spec, and is copied here word for word:

> No warning signs found. Still don't enter passwords or bank details from a message you weren't expecting.

Sources: [G3], [F1].

## Suspicious

| Line | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| Don't click the link and don't reply. | [G1], [G3], [C1] |
| If the message might be real, contact the company using a phone number or website you find yourself. | [G3], [F1], [N1] |
| Watch for pressure: messages that rush you, frighten you or pretend to be someone official. | [F1], [N1], [G1] |
| Then delete the message. | [G1], [N1] |

## High risk

All the Suspicious lines, plus:

| Line | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| Report the message at your local Garda station. | [N1], [G2], [G3] |
| If a number keeps texting or calling you, ask your mobile provider to block it. | [CR1] |

## If you already clicked

Shown under every result.

| Situation | Line | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You gave bank or card details | Ring your bank straight away, using the number on the back of your card. | [F1], [N1], [U1] |
| You typed a password | Change it, and change it on any other account that uses the same password. | [N1], [U2] |
| You opened a link or installed something | Run a full antivirus scan. | [N1], [U2] |
| You lost money or gave details | Report it to your local Garda station, and bring copies of the messages. | [G2], [N1] |
| It was on a work device | Tell your IT department. | [N1], [U2] |

## Where to get help: Irish reporting routes

| Situation | Where to go | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Money was taken, or you shared bank details | Your bank, straight away | [F1], [G3], [N1] |
| Any scam email, text or call, whether it worked or not | Your local Garda station | [N1], [G2], [G3] |
| The scam happened on a website or marketplace | That website's own reporting tool, as well as the Gardaí | [G2] |
| A scam email | Your email provider's "report phishing" option, if it has one | [N1] |
| Scam calls or texts that keep coming | Your mobile provider. Ask them to block the number | [CR1] |

## Checked at source

The research notes marked these as unconfirmed. Each was checked on 12 November 2026.

| Advice | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Factory-reset your phone if you clicked a link or installed an app | **Left out.** NCSC Ireland's guide [N1] does not say this. It says to run a full antivirus scan. No other source read says it either. |
| Use your email provider's "report phishing" button | **Kept, on the help page only.** NCSC Ireland's guide [N1] says most well-known email providers have a reporting service and to check for a "report phishing" option. |
| Report to NCSC Ireland by email | **Left out.** The NCSC Ireland pages read send the public to their local Garda station, not to NCSC. |

## Never shown

- **Texting 7726 and emailing report@phishing.gov.uk.** These are UK-only routes. No Irish source read gives a short code or an address to forward scam messages to.
- **Google's Safe Browsing report form.** It is not an Irish reporting route.
